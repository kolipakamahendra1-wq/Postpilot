# Held Breath

A movement about the interval between motion and consent. Everything in the composition travels in one direction as an orderly procession of identical marks, and everything is defined by the single place where that procession stops. The gap is the subject. Repetition builds the field, and one interruption gives it meaning.

Form is reduced to the rectangle, the line and the dot, repeated until the repetition becomes a texture and then a landscape. Marks are spaced on a rigorous module, so that any deviation, a mark held back or a mark ahead of its neighbours, reads at once as an event. Space is not left over; it is measured, and the empty column before the gate is as deliberate as any drawn element. This is meticulously crafted work, the product of deep expertise: every interval was set by hand and set again.

Colour is a ration. A cool paper ground and a single ink carry almost everything; one vermilion, the red of a rubber stamp, appears only where a decision is required, and nowhere else. Scale stays quiet. Type is small, monospaced and clinical, set like the margin notes of a field instrument, never raised above the marks it annotates. The final surface should look as though countless hours were spent aligning it, painstaking and master-level, with no flourish that was not first questioned and removed.

Composition rests on asymmetry with a fixed axis. The gate sits off centre, the procession accelerates away from it, and the eye is made to wait exactly where the marks wait. Hierarchy comes from interruption, not size.
